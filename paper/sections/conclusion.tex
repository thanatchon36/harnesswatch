\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\answers{Summarises the answers to RQ1--RQ7}

The harness is where an agent's capabilities become effects. Among the publicly disclosed harness-level advisories of 2025--2026, flawed approval checks and configuration honoured before consent account for \pctRCTwoThree{}, and pure prompt injection, a residual class, for \pctRCOne{}. Across \nHarnesses{} harnesses and runtimes, controls exist but are seldom safe by default: sandboxing and egress are the weakest dimensions, headless modes frequently bypass trust gates, and in our sample of public repositories unpinned MCP launches and write-scoped agent CI jobs recur. For closed harnesses, \nClaimsMatch{} of the \nClaimsTested{} testable claims matched, but most documented claims could not be tested headless, and documented features such as instruction-file imports can carry secrets into transcripts without any model action.

The remedies are engineering: trust gates that cover every executable class of configuration and apply headless, permission modes locked by policy, default sandboxes and egress allowlists. We release \tool{}, the rubric, the evidence and the results so that the study can be repeated as the field moves.
