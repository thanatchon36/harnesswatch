\section{Governance Recommendations}\label{sec:governance}
\answers{RQ6 (as recommendations, not an evaluated finding)}

We turned the findings into a 30-control checklist (GC-01 to GC-30). Each control states the requirement, its rationale (threat classes, root causes, rubric dimensions, and whether it addresses a vulnerability, design weakness or policy choice), how to verify it with an exact \tool{} command or a manual check, and its mapping to NIST AI RMF~1.0~\cite{nistairmf}, NIST AI~600-1~\cite{nist600}, ISO/IEC~42001 Annex~A~\cite{iso42001}, OWASP~\cite{owaspllm2025,owaspagentic2026}, MITRE ATLAS~\cite{atlas} and the MAS AI risk-management guidelines~\cite{masairm2026}. The controls that follow most directly from our evidence are: treat repository configuration, including instruction-file imports, as untrusted code and gate changes to it in CI (GC-08, GC-09, GC-23; RC2, E3 secret-in-state); lock approve-everything modes by managed policy and treat allowlists as convenience, not a boundary (GC-06, GC-07; RC3); supply the sandbox and egress boundary yourself, since D02 and D03 score lowest (GC-16, GC-17, GC-20); never run headless agents with secrets or write tokens on untrusted triggers (GC-21, GC-22; E2 CI strata); keep secrets out of config files and the agent's environment and restrict state directories (GC-14, GC-15; E5); and re-run the black-box probes and canary bench on the version and model you deploy (GC-03, GC-04). The checklist has not been evaluated for coverage or usability; the OWASP MCP Top~10 is in beta, ISO/IEC~42001 Annex~A titles come from public listings, and NIST AI~600-1 is cited at document level where needed.
